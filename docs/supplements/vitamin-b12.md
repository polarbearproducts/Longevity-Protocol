# Vitamin B12

## Key Purpose

Vitamin B~12~, also known as **cobalamin**, is a water-soluble vitamin that plays a critical role in several of the body's most fundamental processes. Because the body cannot produce it, we rely on dietary intake (primarily from animal products) or supplementation.

## Dosage and Frequency

* 2000 micrograms per day

## Details

Here is a breakdown of its functions, the risks of deficiency, and the impact of excess intake.

### The Functions of B~12~

B~12~ is unique among vitamins because it contains a metal ion (cobalt) and is essential for two specific enzymatic reactions in humans.

* **DNA Synthesis and Cell Division:** B~12~ is a cofactor for the synthesis of nucleic acids. It works closely with folate (Vitamin B~9~) to create the building blocks of DNA. This is why it is most vital for rapidly dividing cells, such as those in the bone marrow (red blood cells).
* **Neurological Health (Myelin Formation):** It is required for the synthesis of **myelin**, the protective sheath that surrounds nerves. Without B~12~, nerve signaling becomes sluggish or damaged.
* **Energy Metabolism:** It helps convert homocysteine into methionine (an amino acid) and assists in the breakdown of fatty acids and amino acids into energy.
* **Red Blood Cell Formation:** It ensures that red blood cells develop into their proper circular shape and size.

### Complications of B~12~ Deficiency

A deficiency often develops slowly over years because the liver can store a significant reserve. However, once depleted, the effects are serious:

#### 1. Hematological (Blood) Issues

* **Megaloblastic Anemia:** The most common sign. Red blood cells become abnormally large and oval-shaped, making them unable to exit the bone marrow and enter the bloodstream to deliver oxygen.
* **Fatigue and Paleness:** Resulting from the lack of functional red blood cells.

#### 2. Neurological and Cognitive Damage

* **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Tingling or "pins and needles" (paresthesia) in the hands and feet.
* **Balance and Mobility:** Loss of coordination and increased risk of falls due to nerve damage in the spinal cord.
* **Cognitive Decline:** Memory loss, confusion, and in severe cases, "megaloblastic madness" (paranoia or hallucinations).

#### 3. Glossitis

* The tongue may become swollen, red, and smooth (loss of papillae), often making eating painful.

### Excess B~12~: Benefits and Complications

Vitamin B~12~ has a **low level of toxicity**. Because it is water-soluble, the body generally absorbs only what it needs and excretes the rest through urine.

#### Complications of Excess B~12~

While there is no "Tolerable Upper Intake Level" (UL) set by most health organizations, some studies suggest potential issues with extreme over-supplementation:

* **Acne and Rosacea:** High doses of B~12~ have been linked to outbreaks of "B12-induced acne" in some individuals.
* **Kidney Health:** In people with existing kidney disease (especially diabetic nephropathy), very high doses of B-vitamins have occasionally been associated with a faster decline in kidney function.
* **Masking Other Issues:** Excessive B~12~ intake doesn't usually cause harm itself, but it can make it difficult for doctors to diagnose other underlying metabolic conditions if blood levels appear artificially high.

### Vitamin B~12~ Sources

Vitamin B~12~ is primarily found in animal products, though certain fortified plant-based foods are excellent sources for vegetarians and vegans. The **Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)** for most adults is **2.4 mcg** per day.

#### Plant-Based & Fortified Sources

For those on a plant-heavy protocol, these fortified options are the most reliable way to hit daily targets without animal proteins.

* **Nutritional Yeast (Fortified):** This is a powerhouse for vegans. Just **1 tablespoon** typically contains about **2.4 mcg**, meeting 100% of your daily needs.
* **Fortified Cereals:** Depending on the brand, one serving can provide anywhere from **0.6 to 6.0 mcg** (25% to 250% DV).
* **Plant-Based Milks:** Fortified soy, almond, or oat milks often contain about **1.2 to 3.0 mcg** per cup.
* **Nori (Seaweed):** Some studies suggest that eating **4 grams** (about 1.5 sheets) of dried purple laver can provide a bioavailable dose of B~12~, though it is often recommended to use this alongside other sources.

#### Important Note on Absorption

The body's ability to absorb B~12~ from food depends on **Intrinsic Factor**, a protein made in the stomach. While a serving of clams provides a massive amount of B~12~, the body can only absorb a small percentage of very high doses at one time. For maintenance, smaller, frequent doses (like those found in dairy or fortified foods) are often very effective.
